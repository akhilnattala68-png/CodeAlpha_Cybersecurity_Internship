MODULE 01 // FUNDAMENTALS

What is Phishing?

Understanding the deception architecture of social engineering attacks.

Phishing is a form of social engineering where adversaries impersonate legitimate entities—such as banking institutions, streaming services, healthcare portals, or corporate IT teams—to deceive individuals into performing sensitive actions:

  • Disclosing Credentials: Stealing usernames, passwords, API tokens, and session cookies via spoofed login portals.
  • Executing Financial Fraud: Trickery leading to unauthorized wire transfers or fraudulent invoice payments.
  • Deploying Malware: Inducing users to open malicious macro-enabled documents, ZIP files, or executable payloads that establish remote command-and-control (C2) persistence.
The Human Firewall Principle: Modern firewalls, EDRs, and spam filters catch millions of threats, but adversaries constantly innovate evasion techniques. An informed, vigilant human is the final and most critical line of defense.
MODULE 02 // THREAT VECTORS

The 6 Major Categories of Phishing

Adversaries deploy tailored methods depending on their reconnaissance level and intended victim profile.

📧
MASS ATTACK VECTOR

1. Email Phishing (Mass Spray)

Generic, automated mass mailings pretending to originate from well-known brands (Amazon, Netflix, PayPal). Designed to cast a broad net across thousands of users simultaneously.

Defense: Check for generic greetings ("Dear Customer") and verify sending domains carefully.
🎯
TARGETED RECONNAISSANCE

2. Spear Phishing

Highly customized attacks aimed at specific individuals, departments, or companies. Attackers conduct Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) on LinkedIn and social media to reference colleagues, recent projects, and tools.

Defense: Independently verify requests via trusted internal communication channels (Slack/Teams).
👑
C-LEVEL / FINANCIAL FRAUD

3. Whaling (CEO Fraud / BEC)

A specialized subcategory of spear phishing targeting senior executives (CFOs, CEOs, Board members) or impersonating them to coerce employees into approving large wire transfers or confidential data releases.

Defense: Enforce strict dual-authorization controls and verbal out-of-band approvals for financial wires.
📱
MOBILE SMS THREAT

4. Smishing (SMS Phishing)

Phishing via SMS text messages. Common lures include fake package delivery reschedules (FedEx, DHL), bank fraud alerts, or lottery notifications containing shortened malicious URLs.

Defense: Never click links in SMS; manually visit the delivery carrier's official website or app.
📞
VOICE SOCIAL ENGINEERING

5. Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Voice-based impersonation calls. Fraudsters pose as technical support representatives, bank anti-fraud units, or law enforcement to intimidate victims into disclosing one-time passwords (OTPs) or installing remote desktop agents.

Defense: Hang up immediately. Call back through official published customer service phone numbers.
📋
AUTHENTIC TEMPLATE SPOOF

6. Clone Phishing

Attackers duplicate an authentic, previously delivered email (such as a receipt, routine update, or calendar invite) and replace original links or attachments with malicious counterparts, claiming it is an "updated" version.

Defense: Inspect the sender domain and check whether an unexpected revised attachment is truly warranted.

Anatomy of Deceptive URLs & Fake Sites

How cybercriminals construct lookalike domains and why the HTTPS padlock is not proof of legitimacy.

Dissecting a Real vs. Phishing URL

The key to spotting fake links is reading from the first single slash (/) backwards to the left to identify the true registered domain.

✅ AUTHENTIC DOMAIN:

https:// www. paypal.com /signin

🚨 PHISHING SPOOFED SUBDOMAIN (TRICK):

https:// paypal.com.account-verification-security.net attacker-controlled.net /login.php

The Great HTTPS Padlock Myth: A green padlock or "https://" simply indicates the communication between your browser and the website is encrypted with TLS. It does not mean the owner of the website is trustworthy. Attackers obtain free SSL certificates in seconds. Always inspect the exact domain name!
MODULE 05 // SOCIAL ENGINEERING

The 6 Psychological Manipulation Triggers

Phishing works not by hacking machines, but by exploiting universal human cognitive vulnerabilities.

⏳ 1. Urgency

"Action required within 2 hours or access is terminated!" Creates time pressure to bypass critical thinking.

😨 2. Fear & Intimidation

"Legal notice: Your company is facing regulatory fines." Prompts panic reactions to avoid severe penalties.

👔 3. Authority Impersonation

Posing as the CEO, Legal Counsel, or Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) to command obedience.

🎁 4. Greed & Reward

"You've been selected for a $500 Amazon Employee Appreciation Gift Card!" Tempts victims with easy gain.

🔍 5. Curiosity

"Confidential: Q4 Executive Salary Review.xlsx attached." Exploits natural inquisitiveness.

🤝 6. Helpfulness & Sympathy

"Can you please assist me? I'm locked out before the big client presentation." Leverages workplace desire to assist.

MODULE 07 // DEFENSIVE PLAYBOOK

Cybersecurity Best Practices Checklist

Eight non-negotiable defensive habits every cybersecurity professional and employee must uphold.

🔑

1. Deploy Dedicated Password Managers

Never reuse passwords. Password managers refuse to autofill on spoofed lookalike domains.

🛡️

2. Enforce Hardware or App-Based MFA

Use FIDO2 security keys or authenticator apps. Avoid SMS 2FA, which is susceptible to SIM swapping.

🖱️

3. Hover & Inspect Destination URLs

Hover your cursor over links to inspect the destination URL before clicking. Watch for sneaky typos.

📞

4. Out-of-Band Verification for Wires

Always verify financial transfer requests via a known internal phone number or face-to-face.

🚨

5. Report Suspicious Inbound Emails

Click your organization's "Report Phish" button immediately so SOC engineers can block the domain perimeter-wide.

🔄

6. Keep Browsers & Endpoints Patched

Regularly apply OS, browser, and security patches to eliminate drive-by download vulnerabilities.

🔒

7. Beware of Remote Support Prompts

Never install TeamViewer, AnyDesk, or screen-sharing tools prompted by unsolicited phone calls.

🧠

8. Pause Under Artificial Urgency

Whenever an email demands immediate compliance within 24 hours under threat of penalties, pause and reflect.
